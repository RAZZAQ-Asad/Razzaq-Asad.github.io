Selected studies

My work studies how differences between robot controllers and team members affect collective behavior. The studies below use simulation.

JRM 2023 · Journal article

Cooperative transport

Question
Can different controllers improve cooperation among robots with identical bodies?
Method
Evolve neural controllers for object transport; compare one-controller and two-controller teams and three parent-selection methods.
Finding
Two-controller teams performed better in the simulated transport task. This result concerns the tested setting, rather than a general hardware claim.
My role
First author, with Tomohiro Hayakawa and Toshiyuki Yasuda.

ALR 2026 · Journal article

Designing team composition

Question
How many robots should use each controller?
Method
Jointly evolve controller weights and sub-team proportions in a ten-robot transport task; compare with a fixed five-and-five split.
Finding
In Field B, joint design ended with higher average fitness; the best fitness reached a similar level for both methods.
My role
First author, with Toshiyuki Yasuda; part of my doctoral research.

ANTS 2026 · Extended abstract

Learning from specialist controllers

Question
Can one student controller learn from task-specific teachers?
Method
Use knowledge distillation for composite swarm tasks, with task-specific teachers and different training-data amounts.
Scope
An extended abstract on simulated tasks. Continual learning on physical robots remains a future research direction.
My role
First author, with Toshiyuki Yasuda.

Research vision

Research statement

My research asks how to design heterogeneous robot teams whose composition and controllers fit the task. My doctoral work tested joint evolutionary design in simulated cooperative transport. I plan to build on that work through two research aims.

Aim 1 · Robust heterogeneous teams beyond simulation

Extend joint controller-and-composition design toward physical robot teams. The research question is whether the advantage persists under sensor noise, communication loss and robot failure.

Proposed evaluation. Compare joint design with fixed composition under matched resource budgets, using unseen environments, repeated trials and a staged simulation-to-hardware transfer. Cooperative transport and robotic olfaction provide candidate applications.

Aim 2 · Compact controllers that acquire new skills

Build on teacher–student distillation to study multi-task controllers that can incorporate new behaviours while retaining existing skills.

Proposed evaluation. Measure task success, forgetting, compute cost and robustness against specialist controllers and joint-training baselines. Human-facing robot applications would require additional safety and hardware evaluation.

These are proposed research directions. Hardware transfer, continual learning and application deployment are future goals rather than completed results.

Evolutionary Design of Collective Behavior for Heterogeneous Swarm Robotic Systems

My dissertation studies evolutionary design of heterogeneous robot swarms through simulated collective behavior, including cooperative transport and exploration. It brings together my work on neural controllers and team composition.
