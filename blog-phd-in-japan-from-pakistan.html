Doing a PhD in Japan from Pakistan: a practical guide

I came to Japan from Pakistan as a research student, finished my PhD at the University of Toyama and now work in Kyoto. These are the steps and sources I point people to.

Asad Razzaq, PhD4 Nov 20268 min read

I did my Bachelor's and Master's in Computer Science at GC University Faisalabad. In 2022 I came to Japan as a research student at the University of Toyama, and I finished my PhD there in 2025. Today I work as a researcher at Kyoto University of Advanced Science.

Students from Pakistan often ask me how to do the same. This is what I tell them, with links to official sources so you can check the details yourself.

Rules change every year. Amounts, dates and eligibility below are from official pages as of November 2026. Always confirm on the official site before you apply.

1. Start with a supervisor, not a ranking table

For research degrees in Japan, the professor matters at least as much as the university. Many programmes expect you to contact a potential supervisor before you apply, and some require a letter of acceptance from one.

  • Find people, not just departments. researchmap lists Japanese researchers with their papers and projects. KAKEN shows which projects receive government research funding.
  • Read before you write. Read two or three of the professor's recent papers. A specific email (“your 2024 paper on X made me wonder whether Y”) gets a reply. A generic one usually does not.
  • Keep the email short: who you are, why this lab, a one-paragraph research idea, and your CV attached.

2. Know the funding routes

MEXT scholarship through the embassy

The Japanese government (MEXT) scholarship for research students is the best-known route. In Pakistan you apply through the Embassy of Japan in Pakistan.

  • Deadline. For the 2027 intake, applications closed on 26 May 2026.
  • Selection. Complete applications are screened, and shortlisted candidates take a test and an interview in Islamabad or Karachi.
  • Stipend. Under the 2027 guidelines it is 143,000 yen a month as a non-degree research student, 144,000 in a master's course and 145,000 in a doctoral course.
  • Age. Applicants must be born on or after 2 April 1992 (the Embassy of Japan in India lists the same 2027 terms).

The embassy wants a specific set of documents in a specific order: application form, research plan, attested transcripts and certificates, recommendation letters. Read its list twice.

University recommendation and university scholarships

Some Japanese universities nominate international students for MEXT directly, often for English-taught programmes. Many also offer their own scholarships or tuition waivers. Ask the professor you contact which of these their university uses.

JST SPRING, and how it is changing

My doctoral research was funded by a JST SPRING fellowship through the University of Toyama, which supported both living costs and research costs. SPRING is changing for new students, however:

  • Under a new MEXT policy, the living-expense part (the “research incentive”) is limited to Japanese students for those selected from spring 2026 onward.
  • Students who were already supported keep their support for the standard study period.
  • Several universities now state that students with “Student” residence status are not eligible for the living-expense part, while research expenses may still be available.

Examples: Institute of Science Tokyo and Kyoto Institute of Technology. Do not plan your finances around SPRING until you have read your target university's current rules.

Working in the lab

Many labs employ doctoral students as research assistants (RAs). I was one at Toyama. It pays for part of your time and is good experience, but treat it as extra income, not your main funding.

3. Starting as a research student is normal

Many international students start as a research student (kenkyūsei), a non-degree status, for a few months to a year. During that time you join the lab, refine your research plan and then sit the entrance exam for the degree programme.

I did exactly this: about six months as a research student, then the PhD programme. It is a good way to check that the lab suits you before you commit three years.

4. Language and daily life

Many science and engineering graduate programmes in Japan are taught and examined in English, and research papers are in English anyway. Learn basic Japanese all the same. Daily life, lab meetings and friendships all get easier with it.

Living costs vary a lot. Regional cities are much cheaper than Tokyo. Halal food takes some planning outside the big cities, but there are mosques and halal shops in many places, and Pakistani communities are welcoming to newcomers.

5. Make the PhD count

A PhD in Japan is a chance to build an international record, not just a degree:

  • Publish in journals and at international conferences.
  • Apply for travel grants.
  • Look for research visits abroad. My visit to IRIDIA at the Université libre de Bruxelles in 2025 was one of the most valuable parts of my PhD.

Keep your profiles up to date (Google Scholar, ORCID, researchmap) so that people can find your work.
