Artificial Life and Robotics · 2026

Evolving the controllers and the team together

What we changed
We evolved neural controller weights and the number of robots assigned to each controller in the same search.
What we compared
In a ten-robot simulated transport task, we compared joint design with a fixed five-and-five team split.
What we found
In Field B, the proposed method ended with higher average fitness, while the best fitness of both methods reached a similar level.

JRM · 2023

Cooperative transport with different robot abilities

Can robots with identical bodies cooperate better when they use different controllers?

In this first-author simulation study, teams with two evolved neural controllers outperformed teams with one shared controller on the cooperative transport task. The original research clip below shows early and converged behaviour for one-controller and two-controller teams.

ANTS · 2026 · Extended abstract

One compact controller, several tasks

Can specialist swarm controllers be combined into a versatile controller?

This co-authored work studies knowledge distillation from task-specific teachers into a student controller. The method transfers specialist policies; it does not establish general-purpose intelligence.

Interactive

Design a team, compare with evolution

A robot team enters a building it has never seen. Scouts map it with LiDAR and find three heavy packages; workers push them to the dock in crews; everything travels over a radio mesh and every robot has a battery. With a fixed budget, design the team, click any robot to see its sensors, then let a genetic algorithm design its own team. An educational illustration of the team-design question behind my PhD; the demo uses rule-based controllers, not the neural controllers from my papers.
